# Property Relationships

These clusters are suspected connections noted during synthesis, not verified dominance. Every slug listed here is a property in `property-catalog.md`. A property may appear in more than one cluster.

## Ticket 18: failed metadata save followed by a destructor re-flush
Properties: failed-flush-never-reappends-data, stored-transactions-exactly-once-across-resumes, stored-binlog-byte-prefix-of-source, pull-served-stream-self-consistent, pull-chain-prefix-consistency, encrypted-storage-decrypts-to-source, graceful-exit-leaves-flushed-storage
Notes: These share one mechanism. `write_event_block` bumps `size` before `save_binlog_metadata` (`storage_core.cpp:307-321`), and the buffer is erased only on success (`storage.cpp:286-296`), so `~storage` (`storage.cpp:68-77`) appends the same block a second time. `failed-flush-never-reappends-data` is the root-cause guard: its SUT UNREACHABLE fires at the duplicate append. The others observe the consequence downstream. The byte-prefix and exactly-once oracles fail, the served position chain breaks, the CTR offset diverges transiently on S3, and the run can still exit 0. Suspected dominance: every ticket-18 violation also violates `stored-transactions-exactly-once-across-resumes`. In position mode it also violates `stored-binlog-byte-prefix-of-source`. That includes the silent-skip outcome found by the wildcard pass, where resume continues from a valid source boundary. Fixing ticket 18 should clear this whole cluster, apart from other routes into the same oracles.

## Non-atomic multi-object updates and the "next start opens" bar
Properties: kill-anywhere-storage-reopens, interrupted-purge-leaves-openable-storage, stale-index-commit-never-wedges-storage, stored-rotate-or-stop-is-last-event, cli-json-envelope-and-exit-code-agree, recovery-paths-exercised
Notes: Several operations update multiple objects without atomicity: rotation (magic → `.json` → `binlog.index`, `storage_core.cpp:680-703`), purge (index commit → best-effort deletes, `:369-433`) and index rewrites from stale in-memory lists. The strict constructor validators (`:779-783, 968-977, 993-996`) turn every interrupted step into a storage that will not open. Tickets 15 and 9 and lead N3 are the same failure class reached through different operations. The interrupted rotation has a second, silent outcome: in rewrite mode the terminated file is reopened for append (`stored-rotate-or-stop-is-last-event`). An index left empty after an interrupted rotation (ticket 13) feeds the empty-records crash in `cli-json-envelope-and-exit-code-agree`. `recovery-paths-exercised` anchors the recovery branches that these properties rely on. The owners' answer on "unopenable after interruption: bug or fail-stop?" decides the tone of this whole cluster.

## Final flush runs in ~storage after success is decided
Properties: graceful-exit-leaves-flushed-storage, failed-flush-never-reappends-data, sigterm-honoured-within-bound, recovery-paths-exercised
Notes: Both fetch and pull decide their result before the last flush, which runs in `~storage` and swallows every exception. In pull, that destructor can run inside `~io_context` after the `signal_set` is gone. Two kinds of assertion meet at this site. A REACHABLE anchor counts the legitimate swallow after an exception exit. An UNREACHABLE in the operation layer is gated on reported success. `sigterm-honoured-within-bound` shares the shutdown ordering: a slow final S3 flush stretches exit latency. Causal link: a fix that makes the destructor's flush fail loudly changes both the exit-code property and the duplicate-append route.

## No fencing between processes that open one storage
Properties: second-writer-on-storage-fails-closed, other-process-startup-spares-live-writer, stale-index-commit-never-wedges-storage, query-modes-never-write-storage, stored-state-never-regresses, encrypted-storage-decrypts-to-source
Notes: The storage has no lock. `.tmp` names are deterministic. Every non-`querying_only` constructor deletes `.tmp` objects and truncates the tail. Each process works from its own in-memory index. Ticket 24 (two writers), N1/N2 (any opener damaging a live writer), ticket 9 and the concurrent-purge lead (stale index commit), and query modes stamping `metadata.json` are all facets of this one gap. Suspected dominance: `second-writer-on-storage-fails-closed` violations usually also show up as `stored-state-never-regresses` and byte-prefix violations. On encrypted storages the same interleavings break the cipher-offset assertion in `encrypted-storage-decrypts-to-source`. All of these depend on the open contract question of whether concurrent openers are in scope (README:473). After the investigation pass, purge beside a writer is treated as out of contract and runs only in a misuse phase, so `stale-index-commit-never-wedges-storage` rests on concurrent purges. The query-mode stamp looks like an oversight (lead (e) in the catalog).

## Resume and replay correctness
Properties: stored-transactions-exactly-once-across-resumes, inprocess-reconnect-resumes-without-dup-or-gap, gtid-resume-preserves-source-rotations, resume-continuity-guard-never-trips, tagged-gtid-resume-exact, source-upgrade-resume-continuity, source-crash-restart-resume-is-faithful, repeat-fetch-on-caught-up-storage-is-noop, binlog-metadata-matches-stored-bytes
Notes: `stored-transactions-exactly-once-across-resumes` is the end-to-end oracle. The others either name a specific resume route (in-process reconnect, tagged-GTID encoding, an upgrade boundary, a source crash) or check an intermediate condition the oracle depends on. GTID-mode resume derives its request purely from metadata (`binlog-metadata-matches-stored-bytes`), so a metadata/bytes mismatch causes resume errors. Ticket 14 (`gtid-resume-preserves-source-rotations`) is a known violation of byte identity. The exactly-once oracle must filter or track it, or it will dominate every GTID-mode convergence check. `resume-continuity-guard-never-trips` usually fires one resume after an earlier exactly-once violation, so it is a lagging indicator of this cluster. It also fires on a stale in-memory buffer with an intact storage (see the EOF cluster below).

## Lead (a): source EOF mid-transaction skips discarding the buffer
Properties: inprocess-reconnect-resumes-without-dup-or-gap, pull-chain-converges-after-heal, resume-continuity-guard-never-trips
Notes: A graceful source `SHUTDOWN` (issued by the mysqld restart wrapper) or `KILL QUERY` of the dump thread makes mysqld send EOF, possibly mid-transaction. Pull's case (3) at `collector_context.cpp:184-187` returns without discarding the incomplete transaction or flushing, and reconnects with the buffer intact. One root cause, three observers. `inprocess-reconnect-resumes-without-dup-or-gap` owns the root-cause `ALWAYS`s (buffer empty before the GTID request, buffer empty on the EOF path). `pull-chain-converges-after-heal` adds the `UNREACHABLE` at the case (3) return and the workload actions (`KILL QUERY`, `SHUTDOWN` during a large transaction). `resume-continuity-guard-never-trips` sees the consequence: in GTID mode the re-sent duplicates trip the position check, in position mode a partial buffer trips "transaction group must start". Rewrite mode stores the duplicates silently, so only the exactly-once oracle and the SUT `ALWAYS`s catch it there. All three are expected to fire today; one fix (discard and flush on case 3, as case 2 does) should clear the cluster.

## Lead (b): source SIGKILL right after a rotation reaches the ticket 6 path
Properties: source-crash-restart-resume-is-faithful, binlog-metadata-matches-stored-bytes
Notes: In position mode a source SIGKILL right after a rotation leaves N with only FDE + PREVIOUS_GTIDS. On resume the source re-sends them and a fake ROTATE(N+1); `close_binlog` drops the buffered preamble but keeps its timestamps. `binlog-metadata-matches-stored-bytes` catches the wrong timestamp range of N+1 (release) and promotes the `storage.cpp:138` assert, which aborts every retry in a debug build. `source-crash-restart-resume-is-faithful` catches the consequence that N stays magic-only forever, so the storage never becomes an exact copy. Both use the same in-plan fault (the mysqld kill driver plus `FLUSH BINARY LOGS`). A magic-only non-tail file produced this way is also flagged by `chained-fetch-from-pull-reproduces-upstream`.

Notes: All of these go through `sender_context` and `storage_core::fetch_event_block`. `chained-fetch-from-pull-reproduces-upstream` uses PBS's strict reader as an oracle, so it likely detects violations of `sender-file-switch-emits-artificial-rotate` (N5), `dump-preamble-matches-request` and `pull-served-stream-self-consistent` as well. It is a candidate dominating oracle. Two pairs are safety and liveness facets of the same states. `dump-session-ends-with-eof-or-err-packet` requires an ERR packet rather than a silent close. `blocking-dump-ahead-of-flushed-position-eventually-served` and `blocking-dump-before-first-flush-eventually-served` require waiting. ## Single io thread shared by sessions, timers and the signal handler
Properties: sigterm-honoured-within-bound, stalled-dump-client-isolated, pull-chain-converges-after-heal, misaligned-dump-position-rejected-cleanly, stale-sessions-reclaimed-within-bound, listener-survives-fd-exhaustion, rs-unauthenticated-session-never-served
Notes: Every pull session, every timer and the SIGTERM handler run on one `io_ctx.run()` thread. That thread does synchronous storage reads under a storage-wide `shared_mutex`, which the collector takes exclusively for whole-object S3 uploads. A stall on that thread delays signals, write timeouts, pre-auth timeouts and catch-up together. The investigation ruled out reader-starvation of the collector (libc++ `shared_mutex` is writer-preferring, and there is only one reader thread), which invalidated that part of `stalled-dump-client-isolated`; a slow S3 GET on the io thread is the realistic stall. A caught-up client that stops reading is never reclaimed, because the EOF poll never touches the socket (lead (f)), which links `stalled-dump-client-isolated` and `stale-sessions-reclaimed-within-bound`. In release builds, a misaligned dump with `event_size == 0` loops forever on this thread (ticket 7). Causal chain: sessions that are not reclaimed (`stale-sessions-reclaimed-within-bound`) accumulate file descriptors and lead to `listener-survives-fd-exhaustion`. `rs-unauthenticated-session-never-served` shares the same thread: its interleaved and reset-mid-handshake sessions are the same listener-abuse traffic that drives pre-auth reclamation (`stale-sessions-reclaimed-within-bound`), so one listener-abuse driver feeds both.

## Encryption transitions and keystream handling
Properties: encrypted-storage-decrypts-to-source, encrypted-storage-no-plaintext-at-rest, mixed-encryption-storage-served-correctly, ctr-keystream-not-reused-across-truncation, storage-format-version-mismatch-handled, s3-crash-leftovers-bounded
Notes: N9 drives two properties with opposite readings of the same storage. `encrypted-storage-no-plaintext-at-rest` treats a plaintext tail after encryption is enabled as a confidentiality violation. `mixed-encryption-storage-served-correctly` treats it as allowed and checks only readability. The owners' intent decides which one is the bug detector. Keystream reuse and offset divergence both arise from tail truncation followed by re-append under the same envelope. An older binary that ignores the encryption key appends plaintext to an encrypted tail. Leaked S3 local-buffer copies (`s3-crash-leftovers-bounded`) are an extra at-rest location for plaintext, and a source of old ciphertext for the keystream-reuse oracle.

## Rewrite mode
Properties: stored-rotate-or-stop-is-last-event, rewrite-binlog-chain-valid-across-crashes, rewrite-storage-transaction-sequence-matches-source, rewrite-pitr-by-datetime-matches-source, rewrite-toggle-between-runs-keeps-storage-resumable, rewrite-checksum-none-fail-stop, search-by-timestamp-includes-all-early-files, ctr-keystream-not-reused-across-truncation
Notes: Rewrite has no byte oracle, generates preamble events stamped with PBS wall-clock time (N11), performs local rotation in two steps, and stores none of its settings. Internal validity (`rewrite-binlog-chain-valid-across-crashes`) and payload equality (`rewrite-storage-transaction-sequence-matches-source`) are complementary; neither implies the other. Wall-clock stamps connect the datetime-PITR property (lead (c), expected to fail today), the search-by-timestamp property and the keystream-reuse property (lead (d)), because regenerated bytes differ at the same offset. The rewrite toggle wedge comes from the existence check in `storage_core::open_binlog` and is independent of a ticket 14 fix. The missing restart guard in `stored-rotate-or-stop-is-last-event` and the keystream reuse share one cause: a fresh reader context resumes into the current file without checking its size or last event.

## Source identity, failover and server_id
Properties: source-swap-never-silently-accepted, gtid-failover-no-lost-transaction-merge, gtid-failover-resume-progresses, distinct-storages-shared-server-id-stay-consistent, ctr-keystream-not-reused-across-truncation, source-rewind-never-silently-continued
Notes: PBS records no source identity (`storage_metadata.hpp:35-37`) and sets no replica UUID. A swapped or reset source, a promoted replica, and same-server_id dump eviction all reach PBS as an ordinary reconnect. `gtid-failover-no-lost-transaction-merge` (safety) and `gtid-failover-resume-progresses` (liveness) are two facets of one failover event and are in tension: a fix that fails closed on foreign history makes the liveness property depend on the failover being clean. The keystream-reuse property has a non-rewrite variant reached by RESET with reused binlog names. Added by the gap-fill pass: `source-rewind-never-silently-continued` is the same-`server_uuid` case (the source restored from its own backup, GNOs reused with different content). MySQL refuses the dump only until the rewound source has committed enough GNOs, so a `server_uuid`-based ticket 25 fix leaves it open; the two properties share the "no append after a source-history change" shape but not the trigger.

## Error classification and reconnect liveness
Properties: pull-chain-converges-after-heal, source-auth-survives-auth-cache-flush, inprocess-reconnect-resumes-without-dup-or-gap, distinct-storages-shared-server-id-stay-consistent, source-session-tls-when-configured
Notes: Only errno 2013 is retried mid-stream (`connection.cpp:546-551`). A connect failure is retried every `idle_time`, but authentication without a public key can never succeed (ticket 23). Every reconnect rebuilds the connection from config, which is what the TLS regression guard watches. The investigation resolved the errno mapping: every transport fault (partition, RST, source SIGKILL, `KILL CONNECTION`) reaches PBS as 2013; same-server_id eviction sends ER 1236 and the instance exits; a graceful shutdown or `KILL QUERY` sends EOF (lead (a)); an auth failure is errno 2061, dropped in the catch at `collector_context.cpp:212`. The harness supervisor restarts pull, so "pull exits" is observed from its outcome log; whether an S3 network error during a checkpoint may end pull remains a contract question.

## S3 whole-object re-upload
Properties: pull-chain-converges-after-heal, sigterm-honoured-within-bound, s3-crash-leftovers-bounded, stored-state-never-regresses, kill-anywhere-storage-reopens, s3-whole-object-put-publishes-intended-bytes, failed-flush-never-reappends-data, fetch-converges-after-faults-stop
Notes: Every S3 write re-uploads the whole object from a local temp copy under the exclusive storage lock (ticket 17). Resize downloads, truncates and re-uploads the object. This stretches lock hold times (convergence, SIGTERM latency), leaves local copies and possibly multipart leftovers on every kill, opens stale-PUT reordering windows, and widens the startup-recovery kill window. Added by the gap-fill pass: `s3-whole-object-put-publishes-intended-bytes` asserts the body of each whole-object PUT and the resize round trip at backend-local sites, and feeds triggers to the content oracles of `stored-state-never-regresses`. The S3 data-upload variant of ticket 18 (`failed-flush-never-reappends-data`) is "self-healing" only if that resize is exact. A failed temp-file removal after a successful resize PUT is a spurious failed start that `fetch-converges-after-faults-stop` would report.

## Metadata as the single source of truth
Properties: binlog-metadata-matches-stored-bytes, metadata-size-at-transaction-boundary, stored-state-never-regresses, query-success-never-omits-middle-binlog, search-by-timestamp-includes-all-early-files, stored-rotate-or-stop-is-last-event
Notes: Data bytes are never parsed on open (`storage_core.cpp:785`). Resume, `list` and `search_*` read only `.json` fields. ## Known-defect wedges that mask later checks in a timeline
Properties: kill-anywhere-storage-reopens, interrupted-purge-leaves-openable-storage, source-crash-restart-resume-is-faithful, binlog-metadata-matches-stored-bytes, rewrite-toggle-between-runs-keeps-storage-resumable, rewrite-checksum-none-fail-stop, misaligned-dump-position-rejected-cleanly, large-event-served-or-rejected-cleanly, failed-flush-never-reappends-data, stored-transactions-exactly-once-across-resumes, pull-chain-converges-after-heal, fetch-converges-after-faults-stop
Notes: Added by the evaluation pass. Ticket 15, N3, lead (b), the rewrite-toggle wedge, the checksum-none fail-stop loop, ticket 7's debug abort, ticket 10's reconnect loop and ticket 18's CR_1236 wedge each leave a storage or process that stays broken for the rest of the timeline. The end-to-end oracles (`stored-transactions-exactly-once-across-resumes`, `pull-chain-converges-after-heal`) are then never evaluated or fire for the same reason, so every new finding downstream of the wedge is masked. The validator assertions in `kill-anywhere-storage-reopens` use one message per leftover shape so the wedges stay distinguishable. How the harness keeps exploring after a wedge (avoid-trigger knobs, a detect-and-reset helper, variant builds with fixes) is bias B3 in `evaluation/synthesis.md`. `fetch-converges-after-faults-stop` (gap G4) turns a fetch wedge into a red bounded `Always` instead of silence, so every wedge in this cluster reached through fetch now reports directly; its details bucket the known signatures.

## Bounded liveness evaluated in quiet windows
Properties: sigterm-honoured-within-bound, stale-sessions-reclaimed-within-bound, stalled-dump-client-isolated, pull-chain-converges-after-heal, gtid-failover-resume-progresses, checkpoint-bounds-unpersisted-data, fetch-converges-after-faults-stop
Notes: Added by the evaluation pass. All five use a time-bounded `Always`. Every pull session, timer and the signal handler share one io thread with synchronous storage I/O, so node hang, throttling and S3 latency stretch every bound together. The bounds are therefore evaluated only inside quiet windows (`ANTITHESIS_STOP_FAULTS` plus a harness quiet flag that pauses the harness's own fault drivers), with harness-bounded stimuli and bounds calibrated against the instrumented debug build. Native latency faults drive exploration-only measurements. A failure of one of these in a quiet window is a real starvation finding, not an injected stall. The gap-fill pass added two more: the recovery-point bound of `checkpoint-bounds-unpersisted-data` and the attempt bound of `fetch-converges-after-faults-stop`, both evaluated after `ANTITHESIS_STOP_FAULTS` with the harness quiet flag set.

## Retention on either side of PBS
Properties: pull-retention-cycle-keeps-serving-survivors, source-purge-past-resume-point-fails-closed, interrupted-purge-leaves-openable-storage, dump-session-ends-with-eof-or-err-packet, dump-preamble-matches-request, pull-chain-converges-after-heal
Notes: Added by the gap-fill pass (gaps G5 and G7). Retention happens on two sides. On the PBS side, the in-contract cycle for pull is stop → `purge_binlogs` → restart (`pull-retention-cycle-keeps-serving-survivors`). The purge outcome itself is owned by `interrupted-purge-leaves-openable-storage`, and what a reconnecting client asking for a purged file or an empty name receives is owned by `dump-session-ends-with-eof-or-err-packet` and `dump-preamble-matches-request`, whose purge anchors need the same retention-cycle driver. On the source side, a purge past PBS's resume point must fail closed and repeatably (`source-purge-past-resume-point-fails-closed`); that permanent wedge must be re-seeded or excluded so it does not swamp the timeline. Whether retention may purge past a lagging downstream consumer decides whether a chained wedge is a workload error or an expected exclusion from `pull-chain-converges-after-heal`.

## Recovery-point durability and collector memory
Properties: checkpoint-bounds-unpersisted-data, collector-memory-bounded-by-checkpoint, graceful-exit-leaves-flushed-storage, kill-anywhere-storage-reopens
Notes: Added by the gap-fill pass (gap G3, refinement R27). `checkpoint-bounds-unpersisted-data` was split out of `collector-memory-bounded-by-checkpoint`. Both assert at the end of `storage::write_event` with distinct messages: the backlog form (unflushed complete-transaction bytes below `checkpoint_size`, the durability check) belongs to the new property, and the buffer-size form (including the open transaction, the memory check) stays with the old one. The interval checkpoint fires only on event arrival, which drives both the RPO gap (up to `read_timeout` unpersisted after a burst) and the buffered-memory growth during source pauses. What a kill loses is bounded by the checkpoint; whether the next run opens and whether a graceful exit flushes the rest are the other two properties.

## Convergence checks that keep end-to-end oracles from passing vacuously
Properties: fetch-converges-after-faults-stop, pull-chain-converges-after-heal, stored-transactions-exactly-once-across-resumes, graceful-exit-leaves-flushed-storage, stored-binlogs-replay-to-source-state
Notes: Added by the gap-fill pass (gaps G2 and G4). The exactly-once and complete-copy `Always`s are evaluated only after a final fetch exits 0 or pull converges. `fetch-converges-after-faults-stop` and `pull-chain-converges-after-heal` are the bounded liveness checks that make a wedge visible instead of silent. `stored-binlogs-replay-to-source-state` then judges the converged (or prefix) storage by restore impact, which separates cosmetic byte differences from restore-breaking ones.

## Restore-level oracle over the byte oracles
Properties: stored-binlogs-replay-to-source-state, stored-binlog-byte-prefix-of-source, stored-transactions-exactly-once-across-resumes, rewrite-storage-transaction-sequence-matches-source, stored-rotate-or-stop-is-last-event, encrypted-storage-decrypts-to-source, gtid-resume-preserves-source-rotations
Notes: Added by the gap-fill pass (gap G2). Replaying the stored binlogs into a scratch `mysqld` is a consumer-side oracle. It is strongest exactly where the byte oracles are weakest: rewrite storages (regenerated CRCs and preamble), encrypted storages (decrypted offline), and events after a terminal ROTATE/STOP. Suspected relationship: ticket 14's dropped ROTATE (`gtid-resume-preserves-source-rotations`) breaks byte identity but probably not the replay, while a ticket-18 duplicate breaks the strict `--skip-gtids` variant. How `mysqlbinlog` treats events after a ROTATE/STOP decides whether `stored-rotate-or-stop-is-last-event` violations also fail the replay.
